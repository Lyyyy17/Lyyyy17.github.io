Housing Market Analysis in Taiwan's Six Major Cities

Econometric Research · R · Panel Data 2013–2023

What drives housing prices — and why do the drivers differ so much by region? Using two-way fixed-effects and interaction models on a decade of panel data, we showed that northern and southern Taiwan follow fundamentally different price mechanisms.

Problem

Between 2020 Q1 and 2023 Q1, Taiwan's pre-owned home prices rose 32.7% on average across the six municipalities — up to 49.3% in Tainan. Policy debates treat "housing prices" as one national problem, but if the drivers differ by region, one-size-fits-all policy will miss. We set out to quantify that regional heterogeneity.

Data & Method

  • Panel dataset: quarterly county-level data, 2013–2023, six municipalities (N = 264). Dependent variable: official housing price index. Seven explanatory variables: construction cost, green space per 10k residents, floor-area supply, household disposable income, net migration, births, and entertainment-industry density.
  • Diagnostics first: correlation matrix + VIF screening for multicollinearity (all VIF < 10; moderately collinear variables retained on theoretical grounds).
  • Models: two-way fixed-effects models estimated separately for north vs. central-south, then interaction models (variable × north dummy, and variable × city) to formally test whether effects differ by region.

Key Findings

R² = 0.69 vs 0.15

North model explains prices well; the same variables barely explain the central-south — different mechanisms at work

+3.61 index pts

Effect of one extra hectare of green space per 10k residents on northern prices (p < .001)

Sign flip

Construction cost: negative in the north (land cost dominates ~70% in Taipei), positive in central-south cities

  • Environment quality is a northern premium: green space significantly raises prices in the north; in the land-rich south, parks aren't scarce enough to command one — except Tainan, where concentrated green space shows the strongest positive effect of all six cities.
  • Income is decoupled from prices in Taipei/New Taipei (significant negative interaction), consistent with investment-driven demand and spillover of high-income households to peripheral districts. In Taichung and Tainan, income still drives prices the textbook way.
  • Births raise prices only in the double-north — elsewhere, families face a fertility-vs-housing-cost trade-off that flips the sign.

Business Impact

For policy: northern cities should compete on environmental quality; central-south cities need construction-cost control and income growth. For investors: the models identify which fundamentals actually price in per city — e.g., green-space proximity is a priced amenity in Tainan and the north, but not in Kaohsiung.
