LEO Satellite Communications in the Network & Telecom Industry

Industry Report · Supply Chain Analysis · Strategic Forecasting

A top-down map of the low-Earth-orbit satellite industry: a market compounding at 33.8% through 2031, a launch-cost curve collapsing from $85,000 to a projected $200 per kg, and the two Taiwan supply-chain names best positioned to capture it.

Problem

LEO constellations promise broadband where 5G economics fail — half the population in remote regions still lacked reliable internet access as of 2022. The report asks: how big is this market, what actually drives its cost structure, and where in the value chain can Taiwanese component makers capture durable share?

Method

  • Value-chain mapping: satellite manufacturing → launch → ground equipment → services, with Taiwan suppliers positioned at each node (components, PCB/HDI, antennas, RF front-end).
  • Market sizing & triangulation across MIC, TrendForce, McKinsey and industry association data; cross-checked operator launch plans (Starlink 11,927 / Kuiper 3,236 / OneWeb 650 by 2030).
  • Competitive analysis of the three constellation operators' business models — Starlink (B2C-led, subscriber CAGR 191%), Kuiper (AWS integration), OneWeb (B2B via Eutelsat).
  • Cost-curve analysis: launch cost per kg by rocket generation (Falcon 9 → Falcon Heavy → Starship) as the industry's key economic driver.

Key Findings

33.8% CAGR

Global LEO market: US$5.1B (2023) → US$16.5B (2031)

$85,000 → $200/kg

Launch-cost trajectory as reusable rockets scale — the single biggest unlock for commercial viability

2 stock picks

Compeq (2313) — 90% share of Taiwan's LEO HDI boards; UMT (3491) — sole TW microwave/mmWave specialist

  • Cost, not technology, is the bottleneck: reusability drives launch cost from $85,000/kg toward a projected $200/kg within 3–5 years, unlocking commercial satellite markets forecast to compound at 32.6% to 2030.
  • Taiwan's edge is the de-China supply shift: US operators' non-China sourcing policies make Taiwanese HDI-board and RF-component makers structural beneficiaries, not cyclical ones.
  • High-margin niches matter: LEO-grade HDI boards (8–16 layers) carry gross margins over 2× standard PCBs; direct-to-cell services are forecast to grow from $0.4B to $6.5B by 2027 (36% CAGR).
